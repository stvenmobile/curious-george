# 2026-09-16: deep-scoring goes to top-N ranking; my_interests becomes a bounded, decaying pool

Two related pivots today, in the order they actually happened. Both are
downstream of the same real deep-scoring run recorded in
`notebooks/curious_george.ipynb` on 2026-09-13/14 - three non-fixture
candidates (`goal_arbitration`, `sourdough_starters`,
`the_paris_agreement`) run through the full cheap-entry + deep-scoring
pipeline for the first time.

## Deep-scoring: absolute threshold -> top-N ranking

The first run used the original `prune_ratio_threshold=0.0` default and
pruned all three candidates:

| topic | memorization | generalization | ratio mean | ratio stdev |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| goal_arbitration | 4.204 | -0.063 | -0.015 | 0.0062 |
| sourdough_starters | 2.090 | -0.187 | -0.090 | 0.0034 |
| the_paris_agreement | 1.795 | -0.183 | -0.102 | 0.0092 |

Exactly the risk flagged before building this. Canary-based scoring
measures transfer against a fixed, *unrelated* sibling, not a
domain-matched one (see `canary_topics.py`), and real, isolated content
without a deliberately matched partner tends to show modest negative
transfer almost across the board - the same thing Phase 1's own
`known` category showed. An absolute zero cutoff can't distinguish "no
real difference" from "genuinely worse," and it pruned the whole batch
even though the ranking inside it was real and informative:
`goal_arbitration`'s ratio was an order of magnitude less negative than
the other two (~2.4 stdevs from zero vs. 10-25 stdevs for the other
two - not noise, a highly consistent measurement in all three cases,
stdevs of only 0.003-0.009).

Fix: `run_deep_scoring_pass` now ranks by mean generalization ratio and
keeps the top `keep_top_n`, instead of requiring an absolute cutoff.
One rule stays absolute regardless of rank: an unmeasurable ratio
(`memorization_progress` never even went positive) is always pruned - a
red flag about the trial itself, not just "worse than its peers."
Applying `keep_top_n=1` to the same real numbers above:
`goal_arbitration` survives, the other two don't - the outcome the
ranking was already pointing at, now actually reflected in the
decision. Default `keep_top_n=None` keeps every measurable item (no
relative-rank pruning at all) - there isn't yet enough real data across
enough batches to pick a defensible N as a global default.

## my_interests becomes a bounded, decaying pool

`goal_arbitration` resonated strongly with the declared interests and
clearly outperformed two unrelated candidates in the run above, but
`my_interests.json` itself has stayed a static, fixed list of 5 since
Phase 1. If Piper's actual studied experience should ever be able to
reshape what counts as "resonant" for future candidates, the interest
list needs to be able to grow - within limits.

### The proposal and the pushback

Initial proposal: treat `my_interests` like the pipeline's own
`keep_top_n` ranking - a capacity-bounded pool, new candidates displace
the weakest existing entry if they outrank it. Three open questions
were raised: what makes a candidate promotion-worthy, whether the
original 5 should be protected from eviction, and what signal ranks
existing anchors for eviction (proposed: resonance-activation
recency).

The user's refinement was sharper than the proposal on all three
points:

**Value should track ACTIVE STUDY, not mere resonance-matching.**
Being matched/considered isn't the same as being learned about - an
item that's only ever been noticed should be cheap to evict; one with
genuine study behind it should be harder to displace. This is a real
distinction the original proposal missed (resonance-activation recency
would have rewarded being *matched* often, not being *studied*).

**Decay, not permanence, even for studied items.** If study activity
stops for a long stretch, value should decay back down via periodic
re-evaluation - being studied once doesn't earn permanent protection.
Concretized as: starting value 60, -1 point per day since the last real
study event, floor at 0 (~2 months to fully decay if never revisited).
Studying an item resets its clock back to full value.

**The protected 5 stay protected for now, but not as a permanent
architectural fact.** Explicit note: if genuine curiosity-emulation
ever proves itself, control over the interest list might reasonably
shift toward Piper rather than staying human-fixed forever. Not
something to build toward now - just not something to foreclose by
treating "protected" as an unquestionable constant in the design.
Given a concrete value (99, static) rather than being handled as a
special case - always above the pool's max of 60, so the two are
directly comparable without an infinity special-case.

**The promotion trigger is manual, not automatic - and that's fine.**
No autonomous mechanism decides when to add a new interest. It's
literally "anything the user or Claude suggests," used pragmatically -
e.g. to deliberately add a test entry when the research itself calls
for comparing how core-vs-later-added interests behave. This unblocked
building the mechanism immediately: earlier in this same conversation,
the plan had been to defer implementation entirely until an autonomous
trigger existed (the idle-time research loop, still unbuilt). Once the
trigger was clarified as "a manual function call, invoked deliberately,"
the container itself became simple and well-specified enough to build
today rather than wait.

### What got built

`interest_pool.py` - `InterestPool` holds up to `OPEN_SLOT_CAPACITY` (5)
entries, each a `PoolEntry(name, description, anchor_since)`.
`current_value(entry, now)` computes `max(0, 60 - days_since(anchor_since))`
on demand (not cached - avoids staleness the same way mastery/resonance
already do in `memory_store.py`). `add_interest` appends freely below
capacity; at capacity, a fresh candidate (always starts at exactly 60)
displaces the single weakest current entry only if it strictly
outranks it - a tie (both entries at full value, e.g. both
added/studied the same day) does not evict. `record_study` resets an
entry's decay clock. Persists via the same JSON-under-`data/` pattern
`MemoryStore` already uses.

`my_interests.py` gained `PROTECTED_VALUE = 99.0` and
`is_protected_interest(name)`. `interest_pool.interest_value(name, pool)`
is the unified lookup across both sources - 99 for one of the original
5, else the pool's decaying value, raising if `name` is in neither.

Deliberately NOT built: any wiring from the pipeline (`deep_scoring.py`)
or from resonance computation into `InterestPool` automatically. The
pool exists as a standalone mechanism, called manually, until there's
an actual automatic trigger worth wiring it to.

Tests (fast, no model needed - pure timestamp arithmetic): capacity
filling, decay curve and floor, study-resets-value, duplicate-name
rejection, eviction at capacity (weakest displaced, tie rejected),
save/load round-trip, and the unified `interest_value` lookup across
both sources.

## curiosity_score, study.py, and the curiosity_tools/validation_tools split

Three more pieces landed the same day, in this order: the combined
`curiosity_score` ranking function, a real (not disposable) study
session (`study.py`), and then a full package reorganization prompted
by a step-back "where is this project actually headed" discussion.

### curiosity_score

Combines mastery, resonance, and transferability into one ranking
number - the thing Phase 1 and the interest pipeline were always
building toward, and the last of the three axes to actually get used
together. Not equal partners: transferability (deep-scoring's
`generalization_mean`) is the base value and dominant term, since
Phase 1's own real result is the reason mastery/resonance alone can't
distinguish structure from noise. Mastery only applies as a hard
penalty once something is fully `"master"`-level (no room left to
gain, regardless of transfer). Resonance is a smaller additive boost,
not a gate - a high-resonance candidate that's actually noise should
still score low.

Deliberate, evidence-based choice: uses `generalization_mean` (raw),
not `generalization_ratio_mean` - Phase 1's own `known` result showed
the ratio's stdev is dominated by dividing by a tiny, unstable
memorization denominator when comparing topics with very different
memorization magnitudes, exactly the situation for arbitrary real
candidates with different content and lengths.

Verified against the real `goal_arbitration` deep-score from the
2026-09-13/14 run: score 0.6967, mostly the resonance boost (0.76)
offsetting a mildly negative raw transfer signal (-0.063) - the
intended behavior, personal relevance as a boost on substance, not a
replacement for it.

### study.py - a real, committed study session

Distinct from every LoRA trial before it in one respect worth being
honest about rather than glossing over: like deep-scoring, it reloads
a fresh base model and discards the adapter afterward - nothing
persists between calls. That means calling `study_topic` twice on the
same topic will NOT show rising cumulative mastery; each session
measures "would committed study help, right now," repeatably, not
mastery actually accumulating over calendar time. True accumulation
needs the still-open "does study persist into a shared, evolving
model" question resolved - deliberately not solved here, per
`2026-09-13.md`.

Only runs on `ACTIVE`-status items (survived deep-scoring) - enforced,
not just documented, matching the project's established pattern
(`get_study_facts` raising for control-role entities is the same
discipline). Records the post-study loss into `loss_history` and
updates a new `MemoryStore.mark_studied` field - distinct from
`add_or_update_item`'s own "re-study" semantics, which are about
refreshed content, not a real training session against unchanged
content. Re-deep-scoring after a real study session is a separate,
explicit step, using `run_deep_scoring_pass`'s existing `topics`
parameter (built during the rescoring-policy design, before this
module existed) - no new wiring needed.

### The curiosity_tools / validation_tools split

Prompted by a step-back "where is this project headed" conversation
that also covered eventual `piper_assistant` integration as an MCP
server. Two real architectural differences between how this repo runs
today and how an MCP service would need to run surfaced in that
discussion: current functions take a live model/tokenizer/embedder as
parameters (a library shape, not a service shape - an MCP tool call
can only ever pass plain, JSON-serializable data); and every trial
reloads the base model fresh, deliberately, for clean measurement - the
wrong pattern for a responsive service, which would want one warm base
model with LoRA adapters applied/discarded per call. Decided not to
refactor existing code for this now, but to shape new code with it in
mind - and to make the *boundary* between "will eventually be exposed
as MCP tools" and "only ever validated the mechanism" explicit at the
file-organization level, not just in each module's docstring.

Two new subpackages under `src/curious_george/` (nested, not separate
top-level packages - the user's call, since this reorganization is
mainly for clarity about eventual MCP scope, not an immediate
deployment split):

- `curiosity_tools/` - `memory_store.py`, `embeddings.py`,
  `extractor.py`, `loss_measurement.py`, `lora_finetune.py`,
  `my_interests.py`/`.json`, `interest_pool.py`, `pipeline.py`,
  `canary_topics.py`/`.json`, `deep_scoring.py`, `curiosity_score.py`,
  `study.py`. Only this subpackage would ever need to become an MCP
  tool surface.
- `validation_tools/` - `fictional_entities.py`/`.json`,
  `curiosity_topics.py`/`.json`, `curiosity.py`, `warble_harness.py`.
  Proved the mechanism works; never needed once trusted.

One real wrinkle, not just a mechanical file move: `deep_scoring.py`
(production) imported `summarize_trials` from `curiosity.py`
(validation-only) - a dependency pointing the wrong direction, since
production code can't depend on something that stops existing once
validation is done. Fixed by moving `summarize_trials` into
`deep_scoring.py` itself (its main production consumer) and having
`validation_tools/curiosity.py` import it back from there - a
deliberate, one-directional dependency (validation depending on
production is fine; the reverse isn't), not a duplication.

Mechanical scope: every internal import across all of `src/`, every
test file, and the notebook's import cells - verified afterward with
the full test suite (fast and slow) rather than assumed correct from
the edits alone. `canary_topics.py` was the one file worth a second
look before placing - it reads like validation content but isn't:
deep-scoring needs the canaries to function at all, forever, not just
to prove a one-time hypothesis, so it stayed on the `curiosity_tools`
side.

## Phase 4: real complex content - the length hypothesis was wrong, and mastery-of-broad-topics gets deferred

Before this, everything validated had used either deliberately simple
synthetic content (warbles/quaddles, known/moderate/noise) or
single-sentence real candidates. The five declared interests are
genuinely complex, research-level topics - nothing had tested whether
mastery, resonance, and transferability hold up against real
complexity. Also the first real test of a design request from earlier
the same day: Phase 4 needed to be fully self-contained in the
notebook, runnable without first executing Phases 0-3 - confirmed
working on a real, standalone Colab run.

### The real result

Three candidates: `goal_arbitration` (short, high-resonance, reused
from Phase 2), `goal_arbitration_deep` (long, dense, high-resonance -
real content on goal arbitration in autonomous agents), and
`quantum_entanglement` (long, dense, low-resonance - real physics
content, deliberately unrelated to all five declared interests).

`curiosity_score`: goal_arbitration 0.7126, goal_arbitration_deep
0.3164, quantum_entanglement 0.0422. Resonance discriminates real,
meaningful signal even on long, complex content -
`quantum_entanglement`'s raw transfer was actually less negative than
`goal_arbitration_deep`'s (-0.179 vs -0.316), but it still scored far
lower overall, because its resonance (~0.22) is roughly a third of the
relevant candidate's (~0.63). Smaller separation than the one-sentence
candidates showed (0.76 vs 0.05 in Phase 2) - a longer passage's
embedding averages across more ideas, diluting the signal somewhat,
but the discrimination is real, not just noise.

### The length hypothesis was wrong

Both long candidates were predicted to need multiple real study
sessions to cross from `newbie` to `novice`. Instead both blew straight
past `novice` into `master` (loss ~0.001) in a single session - faster
and more complete than the short candidate. Cause, traced through
`study_topic`'s actual mechanics: it calls `finetune_lora(..., [item.
content], ...)` - a SINGLE-ITEM list. Every one of the 200 training
steps trains on the same fixed text, nothing to dilute across. Phase
0/1's fixtures were different: 8-10 separate facts sharing the same
step budget via `rng.choice`, so no single fact got more than ~20
dedicated steps. Here the full 200 steps hit one target directly, every
time - enough to drive a ~150-word paragraph's loss to near-zero, same
as a one-sentence candidate. Length didn't dilute the effort; there was
nothing to dilute it *with*.

### A real flaw in the experiment's own design

The three-consecutive-sessions design couldn't have shown a genuine
multi-session climb even in principle, given an already-documented
limitation: `study_topic` reloads a fresh base model and discards the
adapter every call, so nothing persists between sessions. Confirmed in
the data - `loss_before` was identical across all three sessions for
each candidate (e.g. 3.3409, 3.3409, 3.3409). Each "session" just
reproduced session 1's result from the same fresh baseline. The
experiment could only ever confirm "one session is enough," repeated
three times - not test what it was designed to test. Worth recording as
an honest miss in the experiment design, not just a surprising result.

### The real implication, and the mastery-deferral decision

If any topic saturates to `master` within one session, `curiosity_score`'s
`MASTERY_PENALTY` kicks in almost immediately after a topic's first
real study, regardless of whether the subject is one paragraph or an
entire field - a real mismatch between "mastery of this fixed text" and
"mastery of a broad research topic."

Considered fix: let a topic's content grow into a list of facts/
passages instead of staying one fixed string, reusing `finetune_lora`'s
existing `rng.choice` dilution (already how Phase 0's fixtures behaved)
so mastery growth naturally slows as more material accumulates - no new
training mechanism needed, just a schema change.

Decided to defer this, for two reasons that turned out to be the same
underlying gap, not two separate problems:

1. **Mastery of a genuinely broad, real-world topic can't be measured
   without actually studying it via real sources.** The
   warbles/quaddles trick doesn't transfer here - it existed
   specifically to eliminate pretraining leakage for validating the
   measurement mechanism itself, not to stand in for genuine open
   research questions. Real study requires real search and retrieval,
   which curious-george doesn't have and reasonably belongs to
   `piper_assistant` integration.
2. **A bare accumulated-fact count can't distinguish reliable,
   corroborated facts from confidently-wrong ones.** Solving that also
   requires infrastructure that doesn't exist yet - source credibility
   assessment, or cross-source corroboration (notably, close to what
   the token-coverage idea was originally reaching for, just aimed at
   reliability rather than coverage). Same missing capability as (1),
   not a separate one.

Mastery-of-broad-topics is deferred entirely until `piper_assistant`
integration brings real search/retrieval into scope - not something to
patch or approximate now. Nothing from Phase 4 is invalidated by this:
the loss-based mastery mechanism, resonance discrimination, and
`curiosity_score`'s combination logic all still hold up as real,
validated results. What's explicitly out of scope is "what mastery
means for an open-ended research topic" - left open rather than
answered ungrounded.
